Text Your Ex Back vs Hook Your Ex

Text Your Ex Back is a phased, multimedia texting system from an established relationship publisher: you follow the stage you're in, and the messages follow from that. Hook Your Ex is a compact set of named message templates built on hypnosis-influenced techniques, and it argues openly against the no-contact rule—its templates are meant to restart contact early, not after a waiting period. That difference in approach matters more than the difference in size, and neither is a promise that an ex will return.

The short answer

Choose the learning format—not a promised reaction.

Start with Text Your Ex Back when you want a structured multi-format program that sequences your messages by phase, with separate guidance tracks for women and men. Choose Hook Your Ex when you want a compact set of ready-to-adapt templates and you agree with its premise that silence after a breakup usually works against you. Pause on both if the appeal depends on guaranteed reconciliation, or if your ex has already said stop: no set of texts can override another person's boundaries or decisions, and both programs carry the same ClickBank 60-day guarantee if you decide they're not for you.

Decision guide

Choose Text Your Ex Back when

You want a fuller program with video, audio, and written material, and you prefer working through a phased system—deciding what to send based on the stage you're in—rather than picking isolated templates. The included community access also suits you if you'd rather not do this entirely alone.

Choose Hook Your Ex when

You want something compact you can read and apply the same day, you prefer adapting named templates to your own voice, and you're persuaded by its argument that a long no-contact stretch gives an ex time to move on rather than to miss you.

Pause on both when

You are looking for guaranteed reconciliation, a way around another person's boundaries, or support for abuse, coercion, stalking, crisis, or serious distress. Also pause if your ex has blocked you or asked for no contact: that is information, not an obstacle a template is meant to defeat.

Package details

Two different presentation styles.

Concrete materials matter more than the emotional promises surrounding them.

Digital multimedia program with ebook and video/audio training modules

Text Your Ex Back

A step-by-step multimedia system of specific text messages designed to rebuild attraction after a breakup, with separate tracks for women and men and lifetime community access. The defining feature is sequencing: you don't pick a message off a list, you work through phases, and what you send depends on the stage you're in. That makes it the slower, more deliberate of these two programs—and the one with more to work through if you want structure rather than a script.

By: Michael Fiore / Digital Romance, Inc.

  • Hours of video, audio, and ebook training, all digitally delivered
  • Specific text-message sequences (such as Across the Room and Text Judo) organized into a phased system
  • Separate women's and men's tracks, so the examples and framing are written for your side of the breakup
  • Lifetime access to the Text Your Ex Back online community
  • Product support through Digital Romance's own support desk; order and billing support through ClickBank

Limits to keep in view

  • The seller's best-seller claims and tractor-beam outcome language are marketing, not verifiable outcomes.
  • A phased system only helps if you actually follow the sequence; buying it for one magic message misses the point of the format.
  • No message sequence can guarantee an ex will respond, return, or reconcile.
  • Rebuilding contact after a breakup can reopen conflict; consider whether contact is safe and wanted.

Digital online course with downloadable message templates

Hook Your Ex

A concise digital course built around named message templates, presented as using hypnosis-influenced techniques to prompt an ex to miss you. It was converted from Steve Pratt's seminar material, and its sales page is narrated through one student's story. Its most distinctive position is an argument: the page says the standard no-contact rule backfires because silence gives an ex time to cool off and forget you, so its templates are built to restart contact early rather than after a waiting period. Whether you find that persuasive is the real decision this product asks you to make.

By: Steve Pratt / Grand JV

What the seller lists

  • A full template set, each aimed at a situation: the Remorse Message (for when you've been blocked), Re-ignite, RE-CHARM (for the friend zone), Forgive Me Now, Miss Me Dearly (for long distance), Excite Me Now, LOVE SPARKLE, plus Moody Messages and Mild Seduction messages
  • Compact course in a members area, designed to be read and applied the same day
  • Full text version of the presentation for readers
  • The seller says he personally answers his email—a support claim, not something we can verify

Limits to keep in view

  • Claims about triggering dopamine or obsession chemicals are marketing language, not established science.
  • Overnight-reconciliation promises should be treated as sales copy, not verifiable outcomes.
  • The anti-no-contact stance is the seller's position, not a consensus. If your ex has asked for space, blocked you, or said stop, a template doesn't override that—and sending one anyway is more likely to close the door than open it.
  • No wording can override another person's boundaries or guarantee a response.

A necessary limit

A relationship guide has hard limits.

Neither package can guarantee attraction, commitment, reconciliation, or a particular response. One limit deserves saying plainly because both of these products are about initiating contact: if an ex has blocked you or asked you to stop, that is a decision, not a puzzle. Working around a block—new numbers, new accounts, messages through friends—stops being reconciliation and becomes intrusion. Scripts should never replace honest communication, respect for boundaries, or professional support when safety, coercion, abuse, or serious distress is involved.

Frequently asked

Questions worth answering plainly.

A few direct answers before you open either offer, including format, recurring terms, and the limits of relationship advice.

Which program has more material?

Text Your Ex Back is the larger package, with an ebook plus video and audio training modules. Hook Your Ex is positioned as a compact course built around message templates.

Which is better for someone who prefers reading?

Both offer text versions of their presentations. Text Your Ex Back includes an ebook as part of its core package, while Hook Your Ex publishes a full text version of its pitch.

Do either of these products include recurring charges?

Neither product's core offer was presented as a subscription when reviewed, but always recheck the current order form for preselected upsells or recurring offers before paying.

Can either program guarantee my ex will come back?

No. A messaging guide can organize ideas and examples, but it cannot control another person's feelings, consent, boundaries, or decisions.

Why don't you list prices?

ClickBank sellers change prices and run promotions, so a price printed here could be wrong by the time you read it. The official checkout page always shows the current price before you pay.

How do refunds actually work?

Both programs are ClickBank products with a 60-day money-back guarantee. You request the refund through ClickBank, using the link in your receipt email, within 60 days of purchase; you don't have to persuade the author. Hook Your Ex's seller adds that you can keep the program even if you take the refund.

What shows on my card statement?

Both orders are processed by ClickBank, so the charge appears under a ClickBank descriptor rather than the product's name. That is the name to look for if you're checking a statement.

How do I get the program after paying?

Immediately. Text Your Ex Back is delivered digitally (ebook plus video and audio modules), and Hook Your Ex opens a members area you can log into right after checkout.

What if my ex has blocked me or told me to stop contacting them?

Treat that as the answer. Neither program can override a block or a clear request for no contact, and working around one crosses a boundary rather than repairing a relationship. Both guides are written for situations where contact is still open and safe.
