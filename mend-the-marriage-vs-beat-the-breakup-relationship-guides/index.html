Our quick take

Which one feels right for you?

These two guides point in opposite directions. Mend the Marriage is for repairing a marriage that still exists; Beat the Breakup is for recovering from one that has ended. Same creator, same publisher — the deciding question is which direction you are actually facing.

Best for an ongoing marriage

Best ongoing-marriage fit: Mend the Marriage

It is the bigger, fully itemized package: an interactive ebook, audio course, video series, worksheets, and three bonus books — and both programs are sold through ClickBank with a 60-day money-back guarantee.

See what’s inside Mend the Marriage

Start with the direction you are facing, not the size of the package.

Most comparisons weigh features. This one starts with a fork in the road: Mend the Marriage assumes there is still a marriage to repair, and Beat the Breakup assumes there isn’t. Both come from the same creator, Brad Browning, and the same publisher, so the difference is not who made them — it is what each is trying to do.

Mend the Marriage gets the nod when saving the relationship is still the goal. Its official page itemizes the whole package before checkout: a 270-page interactive ebook, a four-hour audio course, a seven-part video series, team-building worksheets, and three bonus books aimed at specific crises — infidelity, children during a possible divorce, and money conflict.

Beat the Breakup makes more sense when the relationship is over and the goal is to stop the mental loop and move forward. It is the leaner offer: a 240-page ebook (in PDF or MOBI, so it works on an e-reader) and a 10-part video course of just over two hours. Its sales page promises fast relief in strong language; treat that as marketing, and judge the program by its format and focus instead.

Which sounds more like you?

Start with the problem you want to solve.

One is about trying to keep a marriage together. The other is about getting your life back after a breakup.

Pick Mend the Marriage if

You and your spouse are still together — even if things feel difficult or one of you has raised divorce — and you want a structured place to start, including material for specific crises like infidelity or money fights.

Not a match if: the marriage has already ended, or what you need is therapy, couples counseling, or legal advice rather than a self-help program.

Pick Beat the Breakup if

The relationship has ended, you accept that it has ended, and you want help calming the mental loop and getting your footing back. Its whole design — including the “Closure Myth” idea — points away from the ex, not back toward them.

Not a match if: you are still hoping to reconcile (that is a different kind of program), are actively trying to repair a marriage, or need immediate mental health support.

Skip both if: what you actually want is a way to win an ex back — neither program is built for that, and Beat the Breakup points in the opposite direction — or if your situation needs a professional: a therapist, a couples counselor, a lawyer, or safety support. A self-help guide is not a substitute for any of those.

A quick reality check: A guide can give you ideas, but it cannot make a spouse stay or make heartbreak disappear overnight. For abuse or immediate safety concerns, look for qualified local support.
