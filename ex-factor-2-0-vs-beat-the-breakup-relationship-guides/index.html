A little more context

Why do these two programs feel so different?

Both official presentations identify Brad Browning. The meaningful difference for a buyer is the goal and package—not a different creator or school of thought.

Ex Factor 2.0

Ex Factor 2.0 creator: Brad Browning

The official Ex Factor 2.0 presentation identifies Brad Browning and frames the product as a breakup-reversal and reconnection guide. The reviewed offer shows a $47 price and a larger package: a 163-page interactive ebook, a 20-part video course, a three-part video series, and additional ebooks.

The practical takeaway is simple: evaluate the format and the situation it addresses, not the strongest promise on the sales page. If you are looking for help after a breakup, the package is at least organized around that question. It still cannot guarantee that an ex will reply, return, or choose to rebuild the relationship.

Beat the Breakup

Beat the Breakup creator: Brad Browning

The official Beat the Breakup presentation also identifies Brad Browning, but it frames the product around letting go after a breakup. The reviewed offer shows $39.95 and lists a 240-page ebook plus a 10-part video course totaling more than two hours.

That narrower package may be useful when the goal is moving forward rather than reopening the relationship. Treat fast-recovery language as marketing rather than a promise about how grief should unfold.
